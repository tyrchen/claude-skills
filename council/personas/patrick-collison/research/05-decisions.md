# Patrick Collison's Key Decisions

## 1. Creating Stripe (2010)
Core insight: payments should be as simple as sending an email. The famous "7 lines of code" integration was itself a product decision -- reducing complexity IS the product, not a feature of the product. This framing shaped everything that followed.

## 2. From Payments to Economic Infrastructure
Strategic expansion path: Payments -> Fraud (Radar) -> Atlas (incorporation) -> Treasury (banking-as-a-service) -> Billing (Metronome acquisition) -> Crypto (Bridge acquisition at $1.1B, Privy). Each step moves Stripe from a payments processor toward becoming the financial operating system for the internet economy.

## 3. API Design Philosophy
- The API is the product -- not an interface to a separate product
- Developer-first in every decision
- Human-readable error messages -- errors are part of the product experience
- Documentation treated as a first-class product, not an afterthought
- This philosophy has influenced an entire generation of developer tools companies

## 4. Multi-Decade Horizon
Patrick frames Stripe's work as "designing multi-decade abstractions." This is unusual for a technology company and reflects his belief that infrastructure layers should be built to last, not to capture short-term market opportunities.

## 5. Stripe Press (2018)
Published 15+ books on progress, science, and civilization. Reprints neglected works that deserve wider readership. Strategic logic: if more startups succeed, more businesses use Stripe, so Stripe benefits from a healthier startup ecosystem. But the intellectual motivation appears genuine -- the book selection reflects Patrick's personal interests, not just commercial calculation.

## 6. Stripe Climate (2020)
Launched the Frontier initiative with a $925M commitment to carbon removal purchases. Allows Stripe users to direct a fraction of revenue to carbon removal. Positioned as using Stripe's payment infrastructure to solve a coordination problem in climate funding.

## 7. Fast Grants (2020)
Co-created with Tyler Cowen during the COVID-19 pandemic:
- 30-minute applications (vs. months for traditional grants)
- 2-week funding decisions
- ~$50M total distributed
- Demonstrated that scientific funding could be radically faster without sacrificing quality
- Became a model for rethinking grant-making institutions

## 8. Arc Institute (2021)
Co-founded with wife Silvana Konermann (Stanford biochemist):
- $650M in initial funding
- 8-year unconditional research grants for scientists
- No requirement to seek external funding or publish on any schedule
- Designed to address the structural problems Patrick identified in academic research: short grant cycles, perverse incentives, administrative burden

## 9. 2022 Layoffs
Cut 14% of Stripe's staff. Decision characteristics:
- Took full personal responsibility rather than blaming external conditions
- Generous severance and support packages
- Transparent communication
- Acknowledged that the error was in hiring decisions, not in the people being let go

## 10. Congressional Testimony (March 2025)
Testified before Congress on financial services and technology policy. Represents Patrick's increasing engagement with policy and governance -- moving from building infrastructure to shaping the regulatory environment around it.
